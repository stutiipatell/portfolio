CLEVER-VI: Privacy-Preserving Live Remote Assistance

Joint work with Namra Patel, Dr. Ramnarayan Yadav (IITRAM), and Dr. Pragya Verma (Reykjavik University) • Under Review
Lead Author Live · Multi-user

Conventional Remote Sighted Assistance (RSA) requires visually impaired (VI) individuals to share unconstrained, raw camera feeds with remote assistants, exposing sensitive medical records, personal screens, and unconsenting bystanders. CLEVER-VI resolves this through a three-tier, edge-processed live communication architecture where sensitive visual classes are selectively masked on-device before media frames ever leave the user’s local network.

Engineering highlights & user study

  • Privacy-by-construction pipeline: Integrated YOLO object detection, OpenCV multi-stage blurring, and biometric facial authentication on-device to guarantee the authorized user is never masked while third-party bystanders are protected.
  • Empirical field study: Evaluated with 7 completely blind participants across 21 randomized trials (medical prescription reading, key searching in clutter, and medicine bottle identification). 100% of participants reported heightened comfort sharing live feeds due to client-side masking.
Why it matters

Temporal redundancy caching: most frames in a live call barely change, so re-running detection on each one is wasted work. A grayscale mean-absolute-difference check flags near-identical frames, and a SHA-256 content-addressed LRU cache lets CLEVER-VI skip the neural pass entirely on static frames — this is the main lever behind the 7.37 ms mean latency figure above. E-VisWis: Edge-Enabled Assistive Vision & Scene Transformation

Research conducted at IITRAM in collaboration with Reykjavik University • Under Review
Lead Author Offline · Single-user

E-VisWis is an offline, edge-native assistive vision system built for older adults and visually impaired individuals. It bridges the gap between complex visual editing tools and accessibility, allowing users to independently explore spatial surroundings and execute object-level redaction (Hide/Remove) via dual-input voice and tactile commands without remote cloud exposure.

System architecture & HCI evaluation

  • Multimodal interaction loop: Implemented dual-input mode switching with offline VOSK voice recognition prioritized alongside non-blocking keyboard interrupts, avoiding auditory clutter through spatial audio cueing and a 5-second cooldown cache.
  • Field study findings: Evaluated with 21 participants (11 older adults ages 65–85 and 10 individuals with visual impairment). The hide/remove transformation pipeline successfully allowed participants to redact identifying personal documents, credit cards, and screens prior to photo-sharing.
Why it matters

Edge vision ensemble: rather than one general-purpose model, E-VisWis routes each detection task to a specialist: YOLOv8 for COCO-trained common objects, Haar Cascade for a 15 ms facial boundary check, and Tesseract 5.0 OCR for document boundaries. Splitting the work this way keeps every stage fast enough to run entirely offline on modest hardware, which is what makes the 0 KB cloud exposure figure above possible in practice, not just in principle.
